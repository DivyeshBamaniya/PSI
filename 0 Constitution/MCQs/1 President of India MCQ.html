Which article of Indian Constitution is about Term of office of President?

  1. Article 52
  2. Article 54
  3. Article 56
  4. Article 58

Option 3 : Article 56

President of India Question 1 Detailed Solution

The correct answer is Article 56.

Key Points

  • Article 56 of the Indian Constitution deals specifically with the Term of office of the President of India.
  • The term of the President is five years from the date he/she assumes office, according to Article 56.
  • However, the President can continue to hold office beyond the five-year term until a successor is elected and takes charge.
  • Article 56 also mentions that the President can resign from office by submitting a resignation letter addressed to the Vice President of India.
  • It further provides provisions for removal of the President through the process of impeachment, as prescribed in Article 61 of the Constitution.
  • The term can be curtailed if the President is impeached for violation of the Constitution, as outlined in related articles.

Additional Information

  • Article 52:
    • Deals with the establishment of the office of the President of India.
    • States that there shall be a President of India who is the head of state and represents the unity and integrity of the nation.
    • It sets the framework for the position but does not specify the term of office.
  • Article 54:
    • Relates to the election of the President.
    • It specifies the composition of the Electoral College that elects the President, which includes elected members of both Houses of Parliament and the Legislative Assemblies of States.
  • Article 58:
    • Defines the eligibility criteria for the President.
    • The candidate must be a citizen of India, at least 35 years of age, and qualified to be elected as a member of the Lok Sabha.
    • The candidate must not hold any office of profit under the Government of India or any state government.
  • Impeachment Process:
    • The President can be removed through an impeachment process for violation of the Constitution as per Article 61.
    • This requires a detailed procedure involving both Houses of Parliament and a two-thirds majority.
  • Historical Insight:
    • The first President of India, Dr. Rajendra Prasad, served two terms in office, making him the longest-serving President.
    • He assumed office on 26 January 1950 and held the position for over a decade.

Important Points

  • President's Oath: The President takes an oath to preserve, protect, and defend the Constitution of India, as per Article 60.
  • Vacancy in the Office: In case of a vacancy due to resignation, removal, or death, the Vice President acts as the President until a new President is elected.
  • Election Timeline: The election for the next President must be held before the expiration of the current President's term to avoid a gap in leadership.
  • Role of the President: The President serves as the ceremonial head of the state, while the real executive powers are vested in the Prime Minister and the Council of Ministers.
  • Constitutional Safeguard: The President enjoys immunity from legal proceedings for actions taken in the official capacity during the term of office.

President of India Question 2:

Consider the following statements regarding the ordinance-making power of the President of India under Article 123 of the Constitution:

I. An Ordinance promulgated by the President has the same force and effect as a law enacted by Parliament.

II. The President cannot promulgate an Ordinance on a matter on which Parliament itself lacks legislative competence.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. I only
  2. II only
  3. Both I and II
  4. Neither I nor II

Answer (Detailed Solution Below)

Option 3 : Both I and II

President of India Question 2 Detailed Solution

The correct answer is Option C.

Key Points

  • Article 123 empowers the President of India to promulgate Ordinances when both Houses of Parliament are not in session and immediate legislative action is necessary.
  • This power enables the executive to temporarily legislate, subject to later approval by Parliament.
  • An Ordinance promulgated by the President has the same force and effect as an Act of Parliament. However, it is temporary in nature and must be laid before Parliament when it reassembles. Hence, Statement I is correct.
  • The ordinance-making power is co-extensive with the legislative powers of Parliament. Therefore, the President cannot promulgate an Ordinance on matters beyond Parliament’s legislative competence (for example, subjects exclusively in the State List under normal circumstances). Hence, Statement II is correct.

Thus, Statements I and II are correct → Option C.

Additional Information

  • An Ordinance must be approved by Parliament within six weeks from the reassembly of Parliament; otherwise, it ceases to operate.
  • The President acts on the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers while promulgating an Ordinance.
  • The ordinance-making power is sometimes referred to as the “emergency legislative power of the executive.”
  • The Supreme Court in D.C. Wadhwa v. State of Bihar (1987) criticised repeated re-promulgation of ordinances without placing them before the legislature.

President of India Question 4:

In the event of vacancy in both president and Vice president offices, who acts as president?

  1. Attorney General
  2. Prime Minister 
  3. Governor of a state
  4. Chief Justice of India 

Answer (Detailed Solution Below)

Option 4 : Chief Justice of India 

President of India Question 4 Detailed Solution

The correct answer is Chief Justice of India.

Key Points

  • In the event of a vacancy in both the offices of the President and the Vice-President of India, the Chief Justice of India acts as the President until a new President is elected and takes charge.
  • This provision is governed by Article 65 of the Indian Constitution, which specifies the duties of the Vice-President and other authorities in the absence of the President. When both positions are vacant, the Chief Justice assumes this role as a temporary measure.
  • The Chief Justice assumes the role of the acting President to ensure the continuity of governance in the country. This is a temporary arrangement to avoid any constitutional crisis.
  • For example, in 1969, when President Dr. Zakir Husain passed away and Vice-President V.V. Giri resigned, the then Chief Justice of India, Justice Mohammad Hidayatullah, acted as the President until the next President was elected.
  • This arrangement demonstrates the flexibility and foresight of the Indian Constitution in addressing unforeseen vacancies in the highest offices of the country.

Additional Information

  • Article 65 of the Indian Constitution:
    • This article specifies that the Vice-President shall act as the President in the event of the absence of the President due to death, resignation, impeachment, or inability to perform duties.
    • If the Vice-President's position is also vacant, the Chief Justice of India steps in as the acting President.
    • The acting President continues to perform the duties until a new President is elected within six months, as per the provisions of the Constitution.
  • Role of Chief Justice of India:
    • The Chief Justice of India (CJI) is the head of the judiciary system and the Supreme Court of India, which is the highest judicial forum and final court of appeal under the Constitution.
    • In extraordinary circumstances, such as the simultaneous vacancy of the President and Vice-President, the CJI assumes the role of the acting President to ensure governance continuity.
  • Past Instances:
    • In 1969, Chief Justice Mohammad Hidayatullah acted as the President of India after the death of Dr. Zakir Husain and the resignation of Vice-President V.V. Giri.
  • Election of a New President:
    • As per Article 62 of the Constitution, an election to fill the vacancy of the President must be held within six months from the date of the occurrence of the vacancy.
    • The Chief Justice serves as the acting President only during this interim period.

Important Points

  • Attorney General: The Attorney General is the chief legal advisor to the Government of India and does not hold any position related to acting as President.
  • Prime Minister: The Prime Minister is the head of the government and does not act as President in case of a vacancy.
  • Governor of a State: The Governor’s role is restricted to their respective state and does not involve assuming the office of the President.
  • Chief Justice of India: The CJI is the only authority who can act as President in the absence of both the President and Vice-President, as per the Constitution.

President of India Question 5:

The Governor who has all the executive power of the State vested in him shall hand over his resignation from the post addressed to who among the following?

  1. Prime Minister of India
  2. Chief Justice of the Supreme Court of India
  3. Chief Justice of the concerned State High Court
  4. President of India

Answer (Detailed Solution Below)

Option 4 : President of India

President of India Question 5 Detailed Solution

The correct answer is President of India.

Key Points

  • The Governor of a state is the constitutional head, and all executive powers of the state are formally vested in the Governor as per Article 154 of the Indian Constitution.
  • However, the Governor holds office “during the pleasure of the President” as per Article 156(1) of the Constitution.
  • If the Governor wishes to resign, he/she must submit the resignation to the President of India, as the appointment and termination of a Governor’s term are under the authority of the President. Hence, the correct answer is President of India.
  • The resignation letter is addressed to the President, as the President acts on the advice of the Union Council of Ministers and is the authority responsible for appointing the Governor under Article 155.
  • The Prime Minister, Chief Justice of the Supreme Court, or Chief Justice of the concerned State High Court do not have any constitutional role in the appointment or resignation of a Governor.

Additional Information

  • Governor's Appointment and Tenure:
    • The Governor is appointed by the President of India under Article 155 of the Constitution.
    • The Governor's term is generally 5 years, but he/she serves at the pleasure of the President, meaning the President can remove or transfer a Governor before the completion of the term.
  • Governor’s Role:
    • The Governor acts as the constitutional head of the state and exercises powers as per the advice of the Council of Ministers headed by the Chief Minister.
    • In special circumstances, the Governor can act independently, such as during a constitutional crisis or when President's Rule is imposed in the state under Article 356.
  • Key Articles related to Governor:
    • Article 154: Vests the executive powers of the state in the Governor.
    • Article 155: Provides for the appointment of the Governor by the President.
    • Article 156: Specifies the terms and conditions of the Governor’s office, including resignation addressed to the President.
    • Article 157: Lays down the qualifications required to become a Governor (e.g., must be a citizen of India and at least 35 years old).
  • Resignation Process:
    • The Governor writes a resignation letter addressed to the President of India.
    • The resignation becomes effective once accepted by the President.

Important Points

  • Removal of a Governor:
    • The Governor can be removed by the President at any time before the completion of the five-year term.
    • This removal does not require impeachment or any formal process, as the Governor serves at the pleasure of the President.
  • Judicial Scrutiny:
    • The Supreme Court in the B.P. Singhal case (2010) ruled that the removal of a Governor cannot be arbitrary and must be based on valid reasons.
    • This provides a degree of protection to the office of the Governor against misuse of power by the Union Government.
  • Examples of Resignation:
    • In 2009, the Governor of Andhra Pradesh, N.D. Tiwari, resigned after allegations were made against him.
    • In 2014, several Governors appointed during the previous government resigned after the change in the central government.

The procedure of Impeachment of the President of India is __________.

  1. Judicial Procedure
  2. Quasi - Judicial Procedure
  3. Legislative Procedure
  4. Executive Procedure

Answer (Detailed Solution Below)

Option 2 : Quasi - Judicial Procedure

President of India Question 6 Detailed Solution

Download Solution PDF

The correct answer is the Quasi-Judicial Procedure.

Important Points

  • The procedure of Impeachment of the President of India is the Quasi-Judicial Procedure.
  • The procedure begins with the introduction of a bill in either house of Parliament.
  • The only condition for the initiation of the impeachment of the Indian president is the ‘violation of the constitution.
  • None of the presidents of India has faced impeachment so far.
  • A quasi-judicial body is an organ of Government other than a Court or Legislature, which affects the rights of private parties either through adjudication or rulemaking”.
  • It is not mandatory that a Quasi-Judicial Body has to necessarily be an organization resembling a Court of Law.
    • For example, the Election Commission of India is also a Quasi-Judicial Body but does not have its core functions as a Court of Law.
  • Some examples of Quasi-Judicial Bodies in India are the Election Commission of India, the National Green Tribunal, and the Central Information Commission (CIC).

Key Points

  • Process of Impeachment of the President of India:
    • When a President is to be impeached for violation of the Constitution, the charge shall be preferred by either House of Parliament.
    • For the motion to be valid, it must be signed by at least one-fourth of the total members of the house where it is introduced
    • In the case of the Lok Sabha, this means that the motion must be signed by at least one-fourth of the total Lok Sabha members.
    • A 14-day notice is given to the President of India.
    • Then, Lok Sabha passes the impeachment charges with a two-thirds majority and sends it to Rajya Sabha.
    • Then, the Rajya Sabha investigates the charges.
    • While Rajya Sabha is investigating the charges, the President has the right to sit in the proceedings.
    • Rajya Sabha agrees to the charges and passes it with a two-thirds majority and the President is removed.

  1. Article 52
  2. Article 61
  3. Article 74
  4. Article 78

Answer (Detailed Solution Below)

Option 2 : Article 61

President of India Question 9 Detailed Solution

Download Solution PDF

The Correct Answer is Article 61.

Key Points

  • Procedure for impeachment of the President: 
    • When a President is impeached for violating the Constitution, either House of Parliament must support the charge. No such charge shall be preferred until it is accepted by not less than two-thirds of the total membership of the House.
    • When either House of Parliament prefers an accusation, the other House investigates or causes the charge to be investigated, with the President having the right to appear and be served during the investigation.
    • If a resolution is passed as a result of the investigation, with not less than two-thirds of the total membership of the House from which the charge was investigated or caused to be investigated, declaring that the charge preferred against the President has been sustained, such resolution would have the effect of excluding the President from office as of the date on which the resolution is passed.

In India Who among the following has the power to pardon, reprieve or commute the punishment of any criminal?

  1. Prime Minister
  2. Attorney General of India
  3. President
  4. Vice-President

Answer (Detailed Solution Below)

Option 3 : President

President of India Question 13 Detailed Solution

Download Solution PDF

The Correct answer is PresidentKey PointsPardoning Power of President

  • Article 72 empowers the President the power to grant pardons, reprieves, respites, or remissions of punishment or to suspend, remit or commute the sentence of any person convicted of any offence.
  • The meaning of these terms is as follows:
    • Pardon: It removes both the sentence and the conviction and completely absolves the convict from all sentences, punishments, and disqualifications.
    • Commutation: It denotes the substitution of one form of punishment with a lighter form of punishment. For example, a death sentence may be commuted to rigorous imprisonment.
    • Remission: It implies reducing the period of the sentence without changing its character. For example, a sentence of rigorous imprisonment for five years may be remitted to rigorous imprisonment for one year.
    • Respite: It denotes awarding a lesser sentence in place of one originally awarded due to some special fact, such as the physical disability of a convict or the pregnancy of a woman offender.
    • Reprieve: It implies a stay of the execution of a sentence (especially that of death) for a temporary period. Its purpose is to enable the convict to have time to seek pardon or commutation from the President.

The maximum duration of an ordinance issued by the president of India can be _________

  1. 6 weeks
  2. 1 year
  3. 6 months
  4. 6 months and 6 weeks

Answer (Detailed Solution Below)

Option 4 : 6 months and 6 weeks

President of India Question 15 Detailed Solution

Download Solution PDF

The correct answer is options 4 i.e 6 months and 6 weeks.

  • Ordinances making power is the most important legislative power of the President.
    • Ordinances are laws that are promulgated by the President of India on the recommendation of the Union Cabinet.
    • Article 123 of the Indian constitution deals with the ordinance making power of President.
    • President can promulgate ordinances when both the house of parliament is not in session.
    • The ordinance must be approved by the parliament within six weeks of the re-assembly.
    • The maximum duration of an ordinance issued by the president of India can be 6 months and 6 weeks(In case of non-approval by the Parliament).
    • President can withdraw an ordinance at any time.
    • A total of 679 ordinances have been issued from 1950-2014.
  • Article 213 deals with the Ordinance making power of the Governor of a state.
